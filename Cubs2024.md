## Building the Connections

Over the past several years, factors like weather, time of day, and post-COVID attendance recovery have shaped the Cubs' home game atmosphere. This article explores:

- Which temperatures draw the biggest crowds?
- Are night games better attended than day games?
- Did attendance bounce back post-2020?

We found that warm days brought more fans out to Wrigley, and night games often had smaller but steadier turnout. COVID left a dent in attendance, but trends seem to be rising back to 2019 levels.

The 2024 Chicago Cubs season has offered an interesting opportunity to dive into and explore how environmental factors can affect the fan turnout at Wrigley Field. Using the game-by-game data from Retrosheet, this analysis breaks down patterns related to temperature, the time of day, and attendance. Our goal is to uncover whether conditions like weather and time scheduling have any noticeable impacts on how many fans show up to support the Cubs. 

Using the visualizations, we have observed that attendance generally peaked on warmer days, with games played around the 90-100°F range bringing out the biggest crowds. This observation is to be expected, as it is an outdoor event. Fans prefer nice days that are hot then those of colder games that may tamper with the experience. The time of day also played a role; however, it was less drastic than the weather. Day games generally saw higher attendance than the night games, but the margin was modest and showed that the fans were still consistently showing up regardless or not of the sun. 

When compared to that off previous years, specifically the covid era in 2020 and 2021. The Cubs home attendance has since shown signs of rebounding back up. This seasons numbers show that attendance numbers from pre-pandemic levels are now within reach, especially when the weather is warmer and the team is playing well. Together, temperature and time show what helps fill the stands.
